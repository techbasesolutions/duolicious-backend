# Ahavah organic social content and cadence plan

Research date: 2026-09-19. All sources accessed 2026-09-19 unless noted. Every figure carries its source and the source's own date. Where two sources disagree, both are shown.

Scope: Instagram (@ahavah.app, professional account), Threads, the Facebook Page, and the Facebook Group, for a marriage-minded, faith-based matchmaking app with about 25 active members and about 120 accounts, run by one person with automation, at 2 to 4 hours a week.

## Summary

- Posting every week matters more than posting a lot. Buffer found accounts that posted in at least 20 of 26 weeks earned 450% more engagement per post than accounts that posted in 4 or fewer weeks ([Buffer, 2025-01-29](https://buffer.com/resources/consistent-posting-study/)). Build a cadence you can keep in a bad week, then add to it.
- For a new account, Reels are the discovery format and carousels are the engagement format. For accounts under 1,000 followers, the median Reel reached 134 views against 56 for carousels and 23 for single images ([Metricool and HypeAuditor via Net Influencer, 2025-10-14](https://www.netinfluencer.com/instagram-carousels-outperform-reels-for-larger-accounts-content-performance-benchmarks-show/)).
- Spotlight face cards are single images, the weakest Instagram format for reach. Use them to build trust and bring people back, and let Reels and carousels do the discovery work. Cap faces at 2 feed posts a week, roughly one post in three, and send extra welcomes to Stories.
- On Threads and in the Facebook Group, replying and prompting beat broadcasting. Threads says replies account for "almost half of the views on Threads" ([Instagram for Creators, Threads page, data H1 2024](https://creators.instagram.com/threads)).
- Meta's recommendation and ad rules treat dating content with extra care, and a face card on a faith-based matchmaking service arguably reveals religion and relationship-seeking. Consent has to be specific, recorded and easy to withdraw. See section 4.
- Keep promotional posts and campaign emails off the owner's lunar Sabbaths and high days, taken from the owner's calendar (section 1.5). Many members keep a Saturday Sabbath and rabbinic feast dates instead, so keep Saturdays light as well. Nobody benchmarks this, but a brand that promotes through a rest day is off-voice for this audience.

## 1. Key findings per platform

### 1.1 Instagram

#### What the ranking system rewards now

- Adam Mosseri, head of Instagram, said "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends," and that "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content" ([Social Media Today, 2025-01-22](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)). Connected means followers; unconnected means people who do not follow you yet.
  - Many blogs claim a DM share is worth "3 to 5 times" a like. No primary source says this; Mosseri only said "slightly more important". Do not plan around the multiplier.
- Since 2024-04-30, recommendations show a post to a small test audience of possible fans first, then widen distribution if it performs, which gives small accounts a fair start ([TechCrunch, 2024-04-30](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/instagram-is-updating-its-ranking-systems-to-surface-more-content-from-smaller-original-creators)).
- On 2026-04-30 Instagram widened its originality rule from Reels to photos and carousels: "If your account primarily posts unoriginal reels, photos, or carousels you didn't create or edit in a material way, your account may not be seen in recommendations to new audiences" ([Engadget, 2026-04-30](https://www.engadget.com/2160560/instagrams-recommendation-algorithm-will-penalize-unoriginal-photo-and-carousel-posts/)). Ahavah's own cards and designs are original. Reposted Scripture art or third-party quote tiles are the risk.
- Mosseri's year-end memo (2025-12-31) argued that the polished aesthetic "is dead" and that raw, real content signals authenticity. Engadget notes these were his views and predictions, not announced ranking changes ([Engadget, 2026-01](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/instagram-chief-ai-is-so-ubiquitous-it-will-be-more-practical-to-fingerprint-real-media-than-fake-media-202620080.html)). A founder talking to camera fits that direction. Glossy stock imagery does not.
- Instagram's own creator hub says Reels make up "over 50% of all time spent on Instagram" ([Instagram for Creators, How to grow, undated](https://creators.instagram.com/how-to-grow)).
- Faces help. A Georgia Tech and Yahoo Labs study of 1.1 million Instagram photos found photos with faces were 38% more likely to get likes and 32% more likely to get comments ([Georgia Tech, 2014-03-20](https://news.gatech.edu/news/2014/03/20/face-it-instagram-pictures-faces-are-more-popular)). The data is from 2014, before algorithmic feeds, so it only shows direction. The same study found that posting more reduced feedback per photo.

#### How each format performs (the sources measure differently)

| Source (date) | Denominator | Carousel | Reel | Single image |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Socialinsider, 2026-02-20](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram), data Jan to Dec 2025, 35M posts | followers | 0.55% | 0.52% | 0.37% |
| [Buffer, 2026-03-05](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/), 52M+ posts, 2025 | reach | 6.90% | 3.31% | 4.44% |
| [Metricool and HypeAuditor, Jan to Jun 2025, via Net Influencer 2025-10-14](https://www.netinfluencer.com/instagram-carousels-outperform-reels-for-larger-accounts-content-performance-benchmarks-show/), accounts under 1K followers | median views | 56 | 134 | 23 |

- The picture holds across all three: carousels earn the most engagement from people who see them, and Reels reach the most people. Buffer puts it as Reels getting 36% more reach but 12% less engagement than carousels (same source).
- Socialinsider's overall Instagram rate was 0.48% in 2025, down 24% year on year (same source). Buffer's median fell from 7.3% to 5.4% on its reach basis. Instagram engagement is falling for everyone, so judge Ahavah against its own trend, not against the market.
- In Socialinsider's smallest tier (1K to 5K followers), the average post drew 1 to 3 comments and 1 save. An account of Ahavah's size should expect single-digit interactions per post. That is normal, not failure.

#### Stories

- Buffer's frequency study excluded Stories because they "are not typically shown to non-followers" ([Buffer, 2025-08-13](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)). Stories keep existing followers and members warm. They do not grow the account.
- Dash Social (2,000+ brands, Jan to Jun 2025) found brands post about 2 Stories a day, with a 68.3% completion rate and a 6.0% exit rate for accounts under 190K followers ([Dash Social, updated 2025-12-19](https://www.dashsocial.com/blog/every-instagram-stories-performance-benchmark-you-need-to-know)).
- The API can publish Story images but "Publishing stickers (i.e., link, poll, location) is not supported" ([Meta developer docs, IG User Media](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media)). Automated Story reshares of Spotlight cards are possible. Link stickers and polls must be added by hand in the app.

#### How often to post

- Buffer, 2.1M posts from 102K accounts: compared with 1 to 2 feed posts a week, 3 to 5 roughly doubled follower growth (2.2x) and gave about 12% more reach per post. 6 to 9 posts gave 3.7x and 10+ gave 5.5x, with diminishing returns. Buffer calls 3 to 5 a week the "sweet spot" ([Buffer, 2025-08-13](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)).
- Socialinsider's average brand posted 8 Reels, 5 carousels and 7 images a month in 2025 (same Socialinsider source). That is a large-brand norm, not a target for one person.
- Instagram recommends "spreading out your posts rather than publishing several back-to-back" (as reported by [Buffer, 2026-03-24](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)). Do not post a welcome card, a roundup and a quote within the same hour.

#### Best posting times (the sources conflict)

| Source (date) | Data | Best windows (audience local time) |
|---|---|---|
| [Buffer, updated 2026-07-14](https://buffer.com/resources/when-is-the-best-time-to-post-on-instagram/) | 9.6M posts, Jan 2024 to Dec 2025 | Thu 9 a.m., Wed 12 p.m., Wed 6 p.m.; Wednesday best day |
| [Sprout Social, 2026-03-31](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-social-media/) | ~2B engagements, ~307K profiles, Nov 2025 to Feb 2026 | Tue 1 to 7 p.m., Wed 12 to 9 p.m.; weekends worst |
| [Hootsuite, 2026](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/) | 1M+ posts, 118 countries | Mon 3 to 9 p.m., Tue 5 to 8 a.m. and 3 to 7 p.m., Thu 4 to 5 p.m. |

The sources agree on midweek and disagree on the hour. Timing is a smaller lever than consistency (Buffer 2026: "The biggest gap in the frequency data isn't between good timing and bad timing. It's between posting and not posting").

**Image format note for Spotlight cards.** In January 2025 Instagram replaced the square profile grid with a 3:4 vertical preview ([Kapwing, 2025](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/instagrams-new-grid-layout-size-and-dimensions-2025/)). The 1080x1080 card is a square, so in a 3:4 grid tile about 12.5% of its width is cropped from each side (geometry, not a study). Keep the name, age, country and chip inside the central three quarters, or add a 1080x1350 (4:5) variant later. The card's text layout already sits in the Wave 3 renderer, so check a live grid screenshot after the first post.

**Links on Instagram.** Caption links are not clickable. Clickable routes:
- the profile link (use a tracked `/s/<key>` link that rotates to the current campaign);
- a Story link sticker, which every account can now use ([Instagram, announcement](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/expanding-sharing-links-in-stories-to-everyone)), added by hand;
- a private reply by DM to a commenter. The API allows "one message" per comment within 7 days, with follow-ups allowed within 24 hours only if the person replies ([Meta developer docs, Private replies](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/private-replies/)). This supports "comment WELCOME and we'll send you the link" without a third-party tool, if built later.

**Trial Reels.** Since 2024-12-10, trial Reels are shown to non-followers first so creators can test ideas without their followers seeing them ([Instagram for Creators, 2024-12-10](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels)). Secondary sources report a 1,000-follower minimum; Instagram's post does not state one. Check whether @ahavah.app sees the "Trial" toggle. If it does, use it for founder Reels.

### 1.2 Threads

- Official guidance: "Higher post frequency is linked to higher views per post, so we recommend posting at least two to five times per week." "Replies account for almost half of the views on Threads." "Video, photo, and carousel posts that also include text have performed significantly better than posts without any text." (Internal Meta analysis, 30 days in H1 2024, [Instagram for Creators, Threads](https://creators.instagram.com/threads); the same tips were posted by @threads on [2024-10-21](https://www.threads.com/@threads/post/DBZbkQHxjeD?hl=en), adding "Posting more often on weekends may help drive higher engagement").
- In November 2024 Mosseri rebalanced Threads to show more posts from accounts people follow, so "unconnected reach go down and connected reach go up" ([TechCrunch, 2024-11-21](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/21/threads-adjusts-its-algorithm-to-show-you-more-content-from-accounts-you-follow)). Reaching strangers through recommendations is now harder. Replies on other people's posts are the way in.
- Buffer's 2026 data: Threads median engagement about 3.6% (down 18% year on year); video 5.55%, images 4.55%, text 2.79%, link posts 2.34%; replying to comments gave a 42% engagement lift, the largest of any platform ([Buffer, 2026-03-05](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/)).
- Links: Mosseri said in June 2025, "Links have been working much better for more than a month now" ([Social Media Today, 2025-06-08](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-says-link-posts-ranked-properly-threads-reach/750126/)). Clicks remain small: Threads sent 28.4M outbound referral visits in June 2025 (Similarweb) and under 0.1% of publisher referral traffic (Chartbeat) ([Engadget, 2025-07-14](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/threads-users-still-barely-click-links-170139103.html)). A widely repeated claim that links "perform 17% better" had no primary source and is not used here.
- Topic tags and Communities: one topic tag per post; Communities launched 2025-10-02 as a worldwide beta across 100+ topics ([Meta, 2025-10](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/10/introducing-threads-communities-find-your-people/); [9to5Mac, 2025-10-02](https://9to5mac.com/2025/10/02/threads-announces-topic-based-communities/)). Tags such as faith, marriage or relationships put posts in front of interested strangers.
- Automation: the Threads API can publish text, images, video, carousels (up to 20 items), link attachments and topic tags, with a limit of 250 posts per 24 hours ([Meta developer docs, Threads posts](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/threads/posts)). Posting can be scheduled. Replies are the valuable part and should stay human.
- Best time: Buffer's Threads peak is 6 to 11 a.m. on weekdays, peaking Thursday 9 a.m. (Buffer 2026, above).

### 1.3 Facebook Page

- Engagement by followers is tiny everywhere: Socialinsider's 2025 average was 0.15% (25M posts, 130,683 Pages, Jan 2024 to Dec 2025) ([Socialinsider, 2026-03-23](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/facebook)). In the smallest tier it measures (1K to 5K followers), the average post got 5 to 8 likes, 1 comment and 1 to 2 shares. Albums averaged 85 views, status and image posts 60, links 20 and Reels 10. It has no tier below 1K, so there is no benchmark for a Page of Ahavah's size.
- Formats cluster closely. Buffer (engagement by reach): images 5.20%, video 4.84%, text 4.76%, links 4.43% ([Buffer, 2026-03-05](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/)). Socialinsider (by followers, 2025): status 0.20%, album 0.18%, Reel 0.18%, image 0.15%, link 0.05%; by Q2 2026 Reels led at 0.13% and status posts fell to 0.08%. The rankings move quarter to quarter. Links finish last in every dataset.
- Meta's own US data, Q2 2026: 98.9% of Feed views "did not include a link to a source outside of Facebook"; Feed views came 20.2% from friends, 14.6% from Groups joined, 14.8% from Pages and people followed, and 36.6% from unconnected recommendations ([Meta Widely Viewed Content Report, Q2 2026](https://transparency.meta.com/reports/widely-viewed-content-report/)).
- From 2025-12-16 Meta tested limiting some Pages and professional profiles without Meta Verified to 2 organic link posts a month, calling it "a limited test" ([Social Media Today, 2025-12-17](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-considering-charging-business-pages-to-post-links/808099/)). No wider rollout was confirmed. Put sign-up links in the first comment or on the card's accompanying post sparingly, and watch for a limit notice.
- Frequency: Rival IQ's all-industry median is 3.0 Page posts a week, the lowest in its six-year series ([Quid / Rival IQ, 2026-03-05](https://www.quid.com/knowledge-hub/resource-library/blog/2026-social-media-industry-benchmark-report)). Sprout's "industry average is five posts per day" ([Sprout Social, 2025-08-27](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/how-often-to-post-on-social-media/)) describes large brands and is not a target here.
- Best times (conflicting): Sprout, Tue and Wed 12 to 8 p.m. ([2026-03-31](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/best-times-to-post-on-facebook/)); Buffer, Thursday 9 a.m. with 8 to 11 a.m. weekdays ([2026-03-25](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-social-media/)); Hootsuite, Tuesday 5 a.m. with a 5 to 8 a.m. window ([2025-11-19](https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-facebook/)). All agree weekdays beat weekends.

### 1.4 Facebook Group

- API: Meta's Graph API v19.0 changelog (2024-01-23) reads "Deprecated the following Groups API permissions and features: publish_to_groups, groups_access_member_info, Groups API", removed from all versions on 2024-04-22 ([Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/changelog/version19.0/)). Group posting is manual.
- A Page can create a Group, be its admin, and post, comment and moderate as the Page, in the app or on the web ([Facebook Help Center](https://www.facebook.com/help/1769476376397128); [act as your Page in a group](https://www.facebook.com/help/215443548960829)).
- Built-in helpers: an automatic intro for new members on their first visit, and a weekly welcome post through Admin Assist, offered only if someone joined in the last 7 days ([Facebook Help Center, welcome new members](https://www.facebook.com/help/android-app/2063362980550205)). Turn both on. They are the closest thing to Group automation.
- What drives participation: a peer-reviewed study of 274 members of three small closed Facebook groups (57 to 127 members) found that how important members felt the group was explained 27% of the variation in engagement, with offline activity adding 2% ([Bar-Ilan, Gazit and Amichai-Hamburger, Information Research 25(3), 2020-09](https://informationr.net/ir/25-3/paper866.html)). Belonging drives posting, so the Group should feel like "our people" and not a notice board.
- Participation inequality: Nielsen's 90-9-1 rule says most members lurk ([NN/g, 2006-10-08](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/participation-inequality/)). Later studies range from 23% lurkers to over 99%. With about 25 active members, expect 1 to 3 regular posters. The founder carries the Group until that changes.
- No primary study gives a Group posting frequency. The recommendation below is judgment, based on the Threads and Page evidence.

### 1.5 A global audience, Sabbaths and feast days

- The main windows in the studies are local time. Ahavah's audience spans US time zones (Pacific UTC-7 to Eastern UTC-4 in September), the Caribbean (mostly UTC-4), the UK (UTC+1), West Africa (UTC+0 to +1) and Latin America (roughly UTC-6 to UTC-3).
- A post at 8 to 9 a.m. US Eastern lands at 1 to 2 p.m. in London and Lagos and 8 to 9 a.m. in the Caribbean. It is 5 to 6 a.m. on the US West Coast, but an Instagram post keeps collecting views for many hours. This single slot covers the most of the audience, and it fits the Buffer (Thursday 9 a.m.) and Hootsuite (early morning) findings.
- An evening slot (6 to 8 p.m. Eastern) serves the US core and the Sprout and Buffer afternoon/evening findings, but lands at 11 p.m. to 1 a.m. in the UK and West Africa.
- Recommendation: default to about 8:30 a.m. Eastern for feed posts; use an evening Eastern slot for one post a week aimed at the US.
- Rest days and feasts: the brand follows the owner's calendar (from the owner's Biblical Calendar Engine account, read from his saved data; not from web sources). It is lunar, with the new moon as day 1 and weekly Sabbaths on lunar days 8, 15, 22 and 29, so its Sabbaths do not fall on fixed weekdays. 2026 dates from the owner's calendar, Gregorian, with observance beginning the evening before where applicable:
  - Confirmed: Passover 2026-04-01; Unleavened Bread 2026-04-02 to 2026-04-08; First Fruits 2026-04-03; Pentecost 2026-07-13.
  - Projected: Feast of Trumpets 2026-09-12; Day of Atonement 2026-09-21; Tabernacles 2026-09-26 to 2026-10-02; Last Great Day 2026-10-03.
  - Lunar Sabbaths this month: 2026-09-19, 2026-09-26, 2026-10-03, 2026-10-10.
- Rules taken from the owner's instruction: no promotional posts or campaign emails on the Day of Atonement or on lunar Sabbaths; Tabernacles and the Last Great Day get warm, community, non-salesy content; no campaign sends during high holy days.
- The audience is split. Many members are likely to keep a Saturday Sabbath and the rabbinic feast dates, which can differ from the owner's by a day or more. Three consequences:
  - Keep feast content warm and general ("Tabernacles greetings to all who are keeping the feast"). Do not state dates as the only correct ones in public posts.
  - Keep Saturdays free of promotional posts too. Weekends are also the weakest engagement days in Sprout's and Buffer's data (section 1.3), so this costs little.
  - From 2026-09-26 to 2026-10-10 the owner's lunar Sabbaths all fall on Saturdays, so the two groups rest on the same days during the starter calendar. They diverge in later months, and each month's calendar needs checking against the engine.
- No study measures any of this. It is a brand-voice judgment. Threads' own tip suggested weekend posting and Hootsuite rated some weekend slots, so use Sunday, when it is not a lunar Sabbath, for weekend reach.

## 2. Recommended weekly cadence

Time budget assumes Spotlight welcome cards, member-of-the-week and the roundup publish automatically once the Growth queue is approved.

### 2.1 Minimum viable version (about 2 hours a week)

| Channel | Per week | Automated | Manual |
|---|---|---|---|
| IG feed | 3 posts | Spotlight welcome or member of the week (1, when there is an approved card); weekly roundup (1) | 1 backlog carousel or quote graphic, scheduled |
| IG Stories | 3 to 5 frames on 3 days | Reshare of each Spotlight card (image only) | 1 link-sticker frame to sign-up; 1 poll or question sticker |
| Reels | 0 to 1 | none | 1 short founder Reel every other week (phone, 15 to 30 s) |
| Threads | 3 posts plus 15 minutes of replies twice a week | 2 scheduled posts (roundup text, a values line) | 1 question post; replies to your posts and to faith and marriage conversations |
| FB Page | 3 posts | Same Spotlight card and roundup as IG | 1 backlog graphic or link post |
| FB Group | 2 posts | none (Admin Assist weekly welcome only) | 1 conversation prompt; 1 share of the week's roundup or a member-of-the-week card with a question |

### 2.2 Stretch version (about 4 hours a week)

| Channel | Per week | Automated | Manual |
|---|---|---|---|
| IG feed | 5 posts | Welcome cards (up to 2), roundup (1) | 1 explainer carousel, 1 founder Reel |
| IG Stories | daily, 2 to 3 frames | Spotlight reshares | Daily poll, question box or "this or that", sign-up link sticker twice a week |
| Reels | 1 to 2 | none | Founder Reel; one "how it works" screen recording, as a Trial Reel if available |
| Threads | 5 posts plus 15 minutes of replies on 4 days | 3 scheduled posts | 2 question or story posts |
| FB Page | 4 to 5 posts | Spotlight and roundup | 1 Reel crosspost, 1 carousel or album |
| FB Group | 3 to 4 posts | Admin Assist welcome | Monday prompt, Wednesday member intro thread, a Sabbath greeting or reflection on the day before each lunar Sabbath, occasional live Q&A |

### 2.3 Rules that hold at either level

1. Never skip a week. If time runs out, post the roundup and one Story and stop.
2. No more than 2 face cards a week on the feed, never two in a row, and roughly one feed post in three over a month. Extra welcomes go to Stories (see section 4).
3. Space posts on the same channel at least 3 hours apart.
4. Reply to every comment within 24 hours. Buffer measured a 21% lift on Instagram, 42% on Threads and 9.5% on Facebook for posts where the account replied ([Buffer, 2026-03-05](https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/)).
5. No promotional posts or campaign emails on the owner's lunar Sabbaths or high days (from the owner's calendar), including the evening before where observance starts at sundown. Saturdays stay light as well.

## 3. Content pillars

Aim for roughly this monthly mix of feed posts: pillars A and C together about 35%, B about 20%, D about 15%, E about 15%, F and G the rest.

### A. New faces and welcomes (automated Spotlight)

- Welcome cards: "Welcome, Miriam (31, Jamaica)." Caption in the brand voice, asking followers to bless or welcome them in the comments. Comments on a welcome card are the easiest engagement the account will get.
- Member of the week: the card plus one sentence the member wrote themselves ("What I'm looking for in a home: ..."), only with their approval of the exact wording.
- Weekly roundup (count-only, matching the Wave 1 decision): "This week 4 people joined from 3 countries." Pair it with a map graphic in the backlog style once there is a larger week to show.

### B. Marriage-minded values and Scripture-adjacent encouragement

- Quote graphics from the backlog: covenant, patience, preparing for marriage, character before chemistry.
- Verses that most of the niche shares (for example Genesis 2:18, Proverbs 18:22, Ruth 1:16, Song of Songs 8:6 to 7, 1 Corinthians 13:4 to 7). Use one translation throughout, and avoid points that split the community: calendar disputes, the Sacred Name debate, debates about the Godhead, and Messianic versus Hebrew Roots doctrine.
- "Questions to ask before courtship" carousels (10 slides): faith walk, Sabbath and feast keeping, children, finances, family roles, where to live.
- Keep Hebrew terms light and consistent with the app (Ahavah, Shabbat shalom).

### C. How it works, trust and safety

- Explainer carousels: "How Ahavah verification works", "Who sees your profile", "How Spotlight consent works and how to leave it" (this post itself builds trust in the face cards).
- Screen-recorded Reels (15 to 30 s) of the app flow, with no real member data visible.
- Safety tips: meet in public, tell a friend, never send money. A pinned post on each channel.
- Values of the service: marriage-minded, no hookups, and how reports are handled.

### D. Community proof

- Countries represented, growth milestones ("we've now welcomed members from 12 countries"), first conversations and first matches when members agree to share them.
- Honest small numbers work if framed as early and intentional: "We're small on purpose. Here's who's joined this month."
- Waitlist history as a story: "You asked for a place like this. Here's what has happened since."

### E. Founder voice

- Monthly or fortnightly short Reels: why Ahavah exists, what is being built, answers to member questions, a behind-the-scenes look at a new feature.
- Threads posts in the first person about building a faith-based service.
- A short monthly "letter from the founder" that also runs as the community email intro (section 5).

### F. Conversation starters (Group and Threads first)

- "What's one thing your future spouse should know about your Sabbath?"
- "Which feast is your family's favourite, and why?"
- "Would you move countries for marriage? Where from, where to?"
- "Share a verse that has carried you in your single season."
- Polls in Stories: "Courtship: parents involved from the start, yes or no?"

### G. Success stories (when they exist)

- Only with both people's written consent, reviewed together before posting.
- Formats: a two-photo card, a short joint voice note over a slideshow, a written testimony carousel.
- Until the first one exists, use "first conversations" counts and member quotes about the experience instead. Never invent or stage one; see section 4 on fabricated testimonials.

## 4. Using face cards well and safely

### 4.1 Frequency, so the feed is not only faces

- Feed limit: no more than 2 face cards a week, never two in a row, and roughly one feed post in three over a month. The roundup is count-only and does not count as a face post.
- Put extra welcomes in Stories, the Group share and the weekly email. Stories reach followers, not strangers (section 1.1), which also lowers exposure for the member.
- A grid of strangers' faces reads as a catalogue. Break it up with brand graphics, so that any 9-tile view of the profile shows at least 5 non-face tiles.
- Faces help engagement in general (Georgia Tech, 2014, section 1.1). The same study found that posting more reduced feedback per photo, which also argues against flooding the feed.

### 4.2 Consent and withdrawal messaging

The Spotlight spec already enforces the hard parts:
- consent to a specific card revision;
- per-card approval by the member;
- a withdrawal that cancels queued rows and removes published ones;
- first name, age, country and one photo only.

The social side should say this in public:
- Every card caption ends with a short line such as "Shared with {first name}'s permission. Members choose to be featured and can leave Spotlight any time." Keep it short so it does not crowd out the warmth.
- Pin an explainer carousel on IG and the Page: what is shared, what is never shared, how to opt out, how fast removal happens.
- When a member withdraws, remove the post quietly. Never post "X has left", and do not announce removals.
- Removal speed: the spec sets 72-hour removal deadlines and an Instagram manual-removal task list. Say "within 72 hours" publicly only if operations can keep that promise. Otherwise say "promptly".
- Reposts and screenshots cannot be recalled. Say so honestly in the opt-in copy, so consent is informed.

### 4.3 Comment moderation

- Turn on Instagram's Hidden Words filter and add a custom list. Include words about appearance, sex, money, contact-me requests, WhatsApp, Telegram, and phone number patterns.
- On Facebook, use the Page's moderation tools to hide comments with the same words.
- Hide (do not debate) comments that rate or sexualise a member, solicit contact, or promote other services. Delete scams. Block repeat offenders.
- Turn off comments on a card if the member asks, or if one attracts two or more bad comments.
- Never move a public comment about a member into a DM thread with that member. Anyone who asks "how do I contact her?" gets a public reply pointing to the app, where messaging, verification and reporting exist.
- Check comments on face cards twice on the day of posting and daily for 3 days. Engagement is concentrated in the early hours after posting (section 1.1), so that is when problems appear.

### 4.4 Avoiding objectification

- Captions describe the person as a member of a community, not as an available partner. Write "Welcome to the community, Miriam", never "Single and ready" or "Look who's available".
- Avoid ratings, "who would you swipe on" polls, and any comparison between members.
- The card shows first name, age and country only, and the spec forbids finer location, bio or intent. Keep captions to the same limits.
- Alternate men and women in member of the week (the spec already alternates).
- Photos should be the member's chosen profile photo: modest, head and shoulders. The admin reviewer can skip any photo that suits the app but not a public feed.

### 4.6 A small number of opt-ins

- With about 25 active members and perhaps a handful of opt-ins, you will run out of faces fast. Never re-post the same member more often than once a quarter. Member of the week skips a week rather than repeating.
- Fill empty face slots with backlog brand content, not with stock photos of couples. Stock faces presented as members would mislead people.
- Ask personally. A short personal message from the founder to the most active members ("Would you be willing to be one of our first Spotlight members?") is likely to convert better than the E1 announcement alone. This is a judgment, not a measured result.
- Show the non-face version of community: counts, countries, quotes (with permission, attributed by first name and country only).

## 7. Metrics and review cadence

With about 25 active members, percentages swing wildly: one extra comment can double an engagement rate. Track counts and trends over 4-week windows, not per post.

### What matters (in order)

1. **Sign-ups and reactivations credited to social.** The Wave 3b per-click receipts on `/s/<key>` links are the only trustworthy source. Conversion figures from before Wave 3b must not be used. Use a distinct key for each channel and slot: IG bio, IG Story sticker, Threads, FB Page and FB Group.
2. **Returning dormant members within 7 days of a Spotlight post or community email**, from the Growth tab cohorts. This tests the owner's hypothesis directly.
3. **Spotlight opt-in rate**: invites sent, approvals, withdrawals. Withdrawals are an early warning.
4. **Sends (DM shares) per reach and saves per reach** on IG. These are Mosseri's named signals and show whether a post travels.
5. **Comments and replies**, counted per week across IG, Threads, Page and Group, with your own replies counted separately.
6. **Follower growth per week**, per channel.
7. **Reel average watch time and non-follower reach share** (IG Insights shows accounts reached split by followers and non-followers).
8. **Group active members** (Group Insights), and new member requests.

### What to ignore

- Likes on their own, impressions and "engagement rate" versus industry averages. The benchmarks in section 1 come from accounts with thousands of followers or more.
- A single post's result. Look at 4-week sums.

### Review cadence

- **Daily (5 minutes, weekdays):** reply to comments and DMs, hide or remove bad comments (section 4), check that scheduled posts went out.
- **Weekly (15 minutes, Thursday after the roundup):** count sign-ups by `/s/` key, returning members, opt-ins and withdrawals; note the top post on each channel by sends plus comments; note one idea to repeat.
- **Monthly (45 minutes):** compare the last 4 weeks with the previous 4 on the ordered list above. Adjust the pillar mix: move one slot a month toward whatever brought sign-ups or returns. Decide whether the stretch version is sustainable.
- **Quarterly:** re-check the policy points in section 4 (Meta's dating and recommendation policies change), refresh the backlog and retire stale graphics.

### A simple test of the owner's hypothesis

For 8 weeks, alternate weeks with Spotlight welcome cards and weeks without (backlog brand content in the same slots). Compare, per week: dormant members returning, sign-ups credited through `/s/` keys, and comments. With this few members the result will be directional, not statistically significant. A consistent gap across all four pairs is still worth acting on.

## Confidence and gaps

High confidence: the platform findings in section 1, the cadence in section 2 and the format guidance. Each rests on a dated source quoted in place, and where sources disagree both figures are shown.

Medium confidence: the content pillars, the starter calendar and the face-card guidance. These are judgement built on the evidence, fitted to an audience this small. No benchmark exists for a faith-based matchmaking app with about 25 active members.

Known gaps, to close before they matter:

- Meta policy for dating services and the privacy questions around publishing members' faces (section 4.5) were being verified against Meta's own policy pages when the research run was cut short. Treat that section as careful reasoning, not as verified policy. Re-check Meta's Community Standards, the dating ads policy and the personal attributes rule before the first paid promotion of a face card.
- Email benchmarks for small community and dating apps (section 5) are thin. Nobody publishes reliable open and click rates at this list size, so the plan sets its own baseline from the first sends rather than a market average.
- Every number here comes from accounts far larger than Ahavah. Use them for direction, never as a target.
- The feast dates in section 1.5 are the owner's own, read from his Biblical Calendar account on 2026-09-19. Several are still projected in that app and can move when the month is confirmed. Re-read before each month.
- Instagram, Threads and Facebook change ranking often. Re-check section 1 quarterly.
